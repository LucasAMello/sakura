What we are building

This is a preservation-first slice. We reproduce what the surviving complete source says, expose suspicious constants for tuning, and let memory-based playtesting decide what needs correction.

Included now

  • Map10 terrain, camera, entry and exit
  • Normal player movement, jump, health and death
  • Default projectile only
  • Red ball, happy spring and parakeet
  • Enemy contact damage, HP, death and small recovery drops

Deferred

  • Weapons 2–7 and weapon switching
  • The two map10 cards/collectibles
  • Pause, saving and complete HUD polish
  • Map11 and later content
  • Prototype map110 restoration

Asset policy: use the loose BMPs in old/5 jogos new sakura first. They match the release pixels and were probably extracted from the 2008 files.dat. If folder 5 lacks an asset, fall back to old/2 Joguito and verify against the archive when practical. The images embedded on this page represent the confirmed folder-5/release versions.

Map10 terrain atlas

Use old/5 jogos new sakura/textures02.bmp, an 80 × 100 loose copy of the release object named TEXTURES01. The corresponding decoded pixels in folders 2–5 are identical; raw BMP hashes differ only because of header metadata. The larger loose textures01.bmp is a different, unused atlas. Magenta is transparency. Godot should import without filtering and convert magenta to alpha.

Port trap: turning token 0 into an ordinary solid TileMap cell would let the player stand on the bottom row. It must be represented as a lethal area or special floor result.

Horizontal feel

Direction changes erase opposite momentum. Speed grows by 0.2 while below 1, then by 1, capped at 4 pixels/tick. Releasing stops immediately—there is no friction tail. The original discards subpixel position during drawing, so the first fractional movement steps do not visibly move the character.

0→0.2→0.4→0.6→0.8→1→2→3→4 max

The unusual jump

The source stores an initial vertical velocity of −2, but applies vertical displacement as velocity × 5. The first jump step is therefore 10 pixels upward. Gravity changes in three phases, and releasing F during ascent immediately cuts the jump.

Middle ascent+0.06velocity × 5
Late ascent+0.04velocity × 5
Fall+0.04, then +0.06, then +0.10velocity × 5; max +10 px/tick

Damage

Enemy contact uses strict rectangular overlap. Damage grants 40 immunity ticks and does not explicitly knock the player back. During immunity the player flickers using damaged/invisible frames. HP reaching zero, or touching the invisible void while descending, triggers death.

Player art alignment: the shipped normal frame is 54 pixels wide. Facing right it starts at the logical X; facing left it is flipped and drawn 10 pixels left of the 40-pixel body.

Default shot

10 × 10projectile body
1 damageto all three map10 enemies
30 px/ticksix checked steps of 5 pixels
4 shotsmaximum simultaneously active

Facing right, the shot starts at player + (40,35); facing left, at player + (−21,35). It checks collision after every 5-pixel substep, so high speed does not simply tunnel through a 20-pixel wall. It impacts walls and enemies and disappears once more than 640 pixels from the player.

The original does a rectangle broad phase and then samples the projectile footprint against non-magenta pixels in the enemy’s current frame—effectively a small pixel mask.

Needs playtesting: firing cadence uses a shared timer plus the number of active bullets, so holding D may refire differently depending on how quickly earlier shots collide or leave range. The literal thresholds are 10 ticks after release and 25 ticks while held with no active shots.

The enemies on map10

Type 1013 instances

Red ball

40 × 40 · 5 HP · 1 contact damage · 4 px/tick

Patrols horizontally at the player’s top speed. It reverses on solid walls and probes about 50 pixels below both edges so it turns before a ledge. One map instance starts rightward; all others start leftward.

Death plays the shared destruction effect and has a 1-in-3 chance to drop a small +1 HP recovery.

Happy spring

40 × 60 idle / 40 × 73 logical airborne body · 6 HP · 3 contact damage

idle 1–39rise 40–59return 60–99

Does not chase horizontally. It faces the player and follows a hand-authored vertical loop, rising about 139.6 pixels above its base before returning and snapping exactly home at the end of 99 ticks.

Placements: (2720,220) · (6480,380) · (7060,400). Death has a 1-in-3 chance to drop +1 HP.

Type 143 instances

Parakeet / PIRIKITO

37 × 33 · 3 HP · 2 contact damage

descend 1–45rise 46–75

Despite looking like a flying enemy, this source gives it no horizontal movement. It bobs down about 101.25 pixels, rises, then snaps to spawn on tick 76.

Placements: (4320,120)t0 · (6100,160)t0 · (6180,160)t−10. The last two are deliberately ten ticks out of phase. It has no recovery drop.

Compatibility update order

enemy / shot scripts→remove inactive→entry / exit lock→input & shot→horizontal move→ascent→jump start / cut→fall→contact→camera

Godot should run this at 60 fixed ticks per second. Code can be cleanly componentized, but collision and jump timing can change if this observable order changes.

Questions only playtesting can answer

  1. Does 240 px/s feel like the remembered maximum run speed?
  2. Does the source-derived jump feel too high, low, floaty, or sharp?
  3. Was variable jump height noticeable when releasing F?
  4. Did contact damage really have no knockback?
  5. Did the red ball move as fast as the player?
  6. Did the spring wait, then rise roughly 140 pixels?
  7. Did the bird only bob vertically, starting by moving downward?
  8. Did the bullet look nearly instantaneous across the screen?
  9. Did holding fire have an irregular lifetime-dependent cadence?
  10. Did the READY/portal entry last roughly 2.17 seconds?
  11. Should the two deferred collectibles appear in the first test build?
